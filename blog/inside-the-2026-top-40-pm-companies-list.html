Inside the 2026 Top 40 PM Companies List: Software, Structure, and Who's Growing Fastest

Every year I ask property managers to tell me how big they actually are. Most years, a couple hundred do. This year, 390+ did, and the extra data changed what I thought I knew about what "big" actually looks like in this industry.

I published the full, searchable Top 40 list on the site already, broken out by state for the first time. This post is the deeper look at what the numbers behind that list are actually telling us, section by section. If you already saw the newsletter version, this goes further into each chart than I had room for there.

This year's list is presented by Boom.

A couple things jump out. Darwin Homes isn't just #1, it's more than double #2: 24,100 doors against Evernest's 12,000. That's a bigger gap than between #2 and #10 combined. And Minneapolis, of all places, has two companies in the top 10 (Renosy by Renters Warehouse and Kleinman). I did not see that coming.

But the list also has to be read next to what's missing from it. Pure HomeRiver was sitting around 40,000 doors as of January (which would've put it at #1 by a wide margin), and Northpoint reported 6,400 last year before losing a large account. Both declined to submit for 2026. That's not a knock on the list; it's just the nature of self-reported data. The published Top 10 is a floor on what "largest" really looks like in this industry, not a ceiling. The biggest, most institutional players are also the ones with the least incentive to hand a competitor their real door count.

AppFolio: 230 companies, 59%. Rentvine: 76, 19%. Buildium: 39, 10%. Rent Manager: 20, 5%. Propertyware: 13, 3%.

The share numbers only tell half the story, though. When you cut it by median door count per platform, the picture flips a little:

Median size by software

Typical (median) door count among companies on each platform. Count in parentheses.

AppFolio670 (230)
Rent Manager575 (20)
Propertyware558 (13)
Buildium400 (39)
Rentvine350 (76)

Rentvine has the second-highest adoption count on the list but the lowest median size, at 350 doors, well behind AppFolio's 670. That tells me Rentvine's growth right now is coming from a broad base of mid-size, fast-growing operators, not from being the default choice of the very largest shops yet. Meanwhile Rent Manager and Propertyware have small adoption counts (20 and 13 companies) but the highest median door counts on the list. Those read like legacy platforms that older, larger, more entrenched portfolios never had a strong reason to migrate off of. AppFolio is the one platform that shows up at both ends (most companies and one of the highest median sizes), which is a different kind of dominance than just having the biggest logo count.

Departmental is still the default: 183 companies, 47%. Pod-departmental hybrid is a clear second at 30% (117 companies). But size tells a different story than popularity does:

Median size by structure

Typical (median) door count by how the company is organized. Count in parentheses.

Pod-Departmental Hybrid700 (117)
Pods (Squads)674 (21)
Portfolio550 (61)
Departmental544 (183)

The hybrid model is both the second-most-common structure on the list and the one with the highest median door count, at 700, ahead of pure departmental (544) and pure portfolio (550). I want to be careful here: this doesn't prove hybrid causes growth. It's just as likely that companies drift toward a hybrid model as they cross a certain size, rather than the hybrid model being what got them there. Probably some of both is true. But at minimum, it means the biggest operators on this list generally aren't running a textbook-pure version of either model by the time they scale.

How Reliable Is Self-Reported Data, Really?

Every number on this list came from the operator, not from a public filing or a database I can independently audit. A few ground rules I hold the list to:

  • Only third-party-managed doors count. Owned units don't.
  • SFR and small multifamily (under 100 units) only, no HOAs, no big multifamily.
  • Based on 390+ submissions for 2026.

I'm sure a few of these numbers are rounded generously, and I have no way to verify every submission independently. But this is the tradeoff with any self-reported industry list: nobody else is tracking scattered-site SFR portfolios closely enough to rank them, and the alternative to asking operators directly isn't a more accurate list, it's no list at all.
